**Abstract:** Summer precipitation over the Tibetan Plateau has experienced a pronounced north–south wetting–drying dipole in recent decades, yet the processes driving this hydroclimatic contrast remain unclear. Here, we combine moisture tracking with a source-resolved decomposition of precipitation changes during 1960–2020 into frequency and intensity contributions. We find that the dipole is driven primarily by contrasting intensity changes in precipitation events dominated by externally sourced moisture, while events dominated by locally recycled moisture play a secondary role, reinforcing southern drying through reduced event frequency. The subtropical westerly jet, whose weakening has been linked to anthropogenic aerosol forcing and the Tibetan Plateau summer precipitation dipole, contributes to the dipole mainly through changes in the intensity of externally sourced events. These findings highlight the value of decomposing precipitation changes by moisture source and event characteristics for understanding regional hydroclimate responses to climate change.
